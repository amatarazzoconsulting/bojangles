# **Bojangles Menu Enhancement and Food Preparation Modernization**

## **Utilizing Existing Ingredients, Expanding Menu Options, and Improving Restaurant Efficiency**

### **1. Overall objective**

The objective of this proposal is to expand the existing Bojangles menu by making better use of ingredients, sauces, meats, vegetables, and preparation equipment that are already available within the restaurant's food service operation.

The emphasis is on introducing additional food combinations without creating an entirely new supply chain or requiring substantial investments in new equipment.

Many of the proposed products can be developed by modifying existing preparation procedures, adjusting portion sizes, and combining ingredients that are already used in other menu items.

The intention is to maintain the existing quality and flavor of the food while providing customers with additional choices, particularly Southern-style meals that complement the restaurant's established menu.

A second objective is to identify opportunities to reduce preparation costs through standardized portions, improved food handling, centralized preparation, and more efficient ingredient distribution.

## **2. Expanding macaroni and cheese using existing ingredients**

The existing macaroni and cheese is one of the strongest opportunities for menu expansion. The cheese sauce has a very pleasant flavor and provides an excellent foundation for additional combinations.

Rather than developing entirely new recipes, the restaurant could introduce several variations by incorporating meats and vegetables that are already available or relatively easy to obtain.

### **2.1 Chopped steak macaroni and cheese**

The restaurant could introduce macaroni and cheese with chopped steak.

The steak should be prepared separately, cut into small pieces, and incorporated into the finished macaroni and cheese.

A suggested serving would include:

- 8 ounces of prepared macaroni and cheese.
- 2–3 ounces of cooked chopped steak.
- Optional sautéed onions.

The steak should be distributed evenly throughout the pasta.

This would create a more substantial product without requiring a completely new cheese sauce or pasta preparation procedure.

### **2.2 Sausage macaroni and cheese**

Cooked sausage could be sliced or chopped into small pieces and incorporated into the existing macaroni and cheese.

The sausage could be prepared on the grill or another existing cooking surface.

A starting portion of 2–3 ounces of cooked sausage would provide a substantial addition without overwhelming the cheese flavor.

### **2.3 Chopped chicken macaroni and cheese**

Chopped chicken would provide another variation.

Chicken already prepared for other restaurant applications could potentially be used, provided it meets the required food safety, ingredient, and preparation specifications.

The chicken should be cut into approximately ½-inch pieces and distributed evenly throughout the macaroni and cheese.

This would provide an additional protein option using a familiar restaurant ingredient.

### **2.4 Additional pasta shapes**

The restaurant should also consider expanding the pasta selection.

Possible options include:

- Traditional elbow macaroni.
- Shell pasta.
- Larger shells.
- Mixed pasta shapes.
- Southern-style holiday pasta combinations.

The existing cheese sauce could be retained, allowing the restaurant to introduce different pasta varieties without substantially changing its sauce manufacturing requirements.

## **3. Extending the existing cheese sauce into other side dishes**

The cheese sauce could also be used to develop additional variations of existing side dishes.

One particularly practical application would be mashed potatoes.

The restaurant could offer cheese-enhanced mashed potatoes by incorporating a measured quantity of the existing cheese sauce into the prepared potatoes.

Other possibilities include:

- Cheddar mashed potatoes.
- Mashed potatoes with chopped sausage.
- Mashed potatoes with chopped chicken.
- Mashed potatoes with sautéed onions.
- Cheese-covered broccoli.

The advantage is that the restaurant would be using a common ingredient across multiple products.

This could reduce the number of separate sauces that need to be purchased, stored, prepared, and maintained.

## **4. Breaded chopped steak cutlet with gravy**

An important clarification concerning the steak product is that the intended item is the traditional thin, mechanically tenderized, breaded steak cutlet, rather than a conventional chopped steak patty.

This is the style of steak that is passed through a mechanical tenderizing machine, producing a distinctive textured surface with a pattern of small indentations or marks.

It is commonly called a **cubed steak**, **minute steak**, or **country-fried steak**, depending on its preparation and presentation.

The mechanical tenderizing process creates a thinner, more tender piece of beef that can be breaded and cooked.

### **4.1 Product preparation**

The proposed product would use a thin beef cutlet that has been mechanically tenderized.

The preparation sequence would be:

1. Receive appropriately specified beef cutlets from an approved supplier.
2. Verify their condition, labeling, temperature, and product specifications.
3. Mechanically tenderize the beef using an approved commercial process, if not already tenderized by the supplier.
4. Apply a seasoned flour coating or standard breading.
5. Cook using the restaurant's established frying equipment and validated cooking procedure.
6. Serve with prepared brown gravy or country-style gravy.

A finished cutlet should be tender enough to cut easily with a fork.

The breading should have a golden-brown exterior, while the beef should remain moist and properly cooked.

Because mechanically tenderized beef can carry surface bacteria into its interior, it must be treated as a non-intact beef product. A validated cooking process should achieve at least 155°F for 17 seconds, or an applicable approved alternative.

### **4.2 Serving combinations**

The cutlet could be served with several existing side dishes:

- Mashed potatoes and gravy.
- Macaroni and cheese.
- Green beans.
- Broccoli.
- Cornbread muffins.

This creates a recognizable Southern-style meal without requiring an entirely new category of food manufacturing.

The gravy could potentially be prepared centrally and delivered in standardized containers.

## **5. Grill preparation: sautéed onions and vegetables**

The grill area could provide additional capabilities through simple preparation modifications.

One particularly useful addition would be freshly sautéed onions.

Onions could be peeled, chopped, and sautéed on the existing flat-top grill.

A practical preparation procedure would use a cooking surface around 300–350°F, with a measured quantity of oil or butter.

The onions should be stirred periodically and cooked for approximately 10–20 minutes, depending on their thickness and the cooking equipment.

The desired result is a soft, golden-brown onion with a naturally sweet flavor.

These onions could be used in:

- Breaded steak cutlet and gravy combinations.
- Macaroni and cheese with steak.
- Sausage macaroni and cheese.
- Mashed potatoes.
- Grilled chicken.
- Additional vegetable combinations.

Other vegetables could be prepared using the same general approach, particularly peppers and mushrooms, if these ingredients are introduced into the purchasing program.

### **5.1 Broccoli side dish**

Broccoli would be a useful addition to the existing side selection.

A standard portion of approximately 4 ounces could be steamed and lightly seasoned with butter, salt, and pepper.

The broccoli should retain its green color and a slightly firm texture.

A second variation could use the existing cheese sauce.

This provides another opportunity to expand the menu without developing a new sauce or an entirely separate preparation operation.

## **6. Six-piece cornbread muffin orders**

Cornbread muffins would complement the Southern-style menu.

The proposed offering would be six muffins per order, with two variations.

**Cheese cornbread muffins**

- Traditional cornbread batter.
- Measured cheddar cheese.
- Golden exterior.
- Moist interior.

**Jalapeño cornbread muffins**

- Traditional cornbread batter.
- Finely chopped jalapeños.
- Optional cheddar cheese.

Each muffin could have a target finished weight of approximately 2 ounces, producing a six-piece order of approximately 12 ounces.

A standardized commercial muffin tray and convection oven would allow the bakery preparation to be scheduled in batches.

The restaurant could use centralized batter preparation or prepared dry ingredient packages to maintain consistency.

## **7. Making better use of existing ingredients**

The guiding principle should be to examine the existing ingredient inventory before introducing additional products.

For example, a restaurant that already purchases chicken, sausage, cheese, potatoes, onions, flour, and gravy ingredients has many of the necessary components to introduce these additional menu items.

The same ingredients can be used in different combinations.

| Existing ingredient | Additional application                   |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Cheese sauce        | Macaroni, broccoli, mashed potatoes      |
| Chicken             | Chopped chicken macaroni and cheese      |
| Sausage             | Sausage macaroni and cheese              |
| Beef cutlets        | Breaded steak and gravy                  |
| Onions              | Steak, potatoes, chicken, macaroni       |
| Potatoes            | Mashed potatoes and cheese potatoes      |
| Flour               | Breading and cornbread                   |
| Gravy               | Breaded steak cutlet and mashed potatoes |
| Broccoli            | Vegetable side and cheese broccoli       |

This approach reduces unnecessary ingredient duplication.

It also makes menu development easier because employees can work with familiar ingredients and preparation techniques.

## **8. Automated preparation and cartridge-style inventory**

The longer-term opportunity is to develop a distribution system that supplies restaurants with standardized ingredients in measured quantities.

The emphasis should remain on the ingredients already used throughout the restaurant network.

For example, instead of every restaurant independently preparing gravy, chopping chicken, measuring cheese sauce, or preparing vegetables, selected products could be manufactured centrally and distributed in standardized food-grade containers.

These could be designed as cartridge-style inventory modules.

Each container would identify:

- Product name.
- Ingredient composition.
- Quantity.
- Production date.
- Lot number.
- Storage temperature.
- Use-by date.
- Preparation instructions.

A restaurant employee could retrieve the appropriate container, scan its identification code, and dispense a measured portion.

The system could support automated replenishment according to restaurant sales and inventory consumption.

Importantly, this should not mean that every ingredient must be fully prepared in a factory. Some vegetables, meats, and other ingredients may be better prepared fresh at the restaurant.

The objective is to centralize only those preparation activities that genuinely improve consistency, cost, and efficiency.

## **9. Centralized manufacturing and agricultural supply**

The larger opportunity is to connect ingredient purchasing, agricultural production, and food manufacturing.

The company could progressively establish direct relationships with agricultural producers and food-processing facilities.

Potential areas include:

- Potato production and processing.
- Green bean cultivation.
- Onion production.
- Broccoli and vegetable cultivation.
- Dairy and cheese supply.
- Flour and grain processing.
- Coffee bean roasting.
- Tea processing and packaging.
- Pasta manufacturing.

An automated potato processing operation, for example, could wash, grade, peel, cut, and package potatoes according to restaurant specifications.

A green bean processing operation could wash, trim, grade, and package vegetables for distribution.

A centralized pasta operation could manufacture elbow macaroni and shells using flour and other approved ingredients.

The purpose would be to gain greater control over raw ingredient costs and product consistency.

Not every agricultural product needs to be grown directly by the company. Some ingredients may remain more economical to purchase from specialized suppliers.

The decision should be based on production cost, transportation, quality, availability, and demand.

## **10. Food safety and handling requirements**

All new products should follow established food safety procedures.

The most important requirements include:

- Cold storage at 41°F or below.
- Hot holding at 135°F or above.
- Properly validated cooking temperatures for chicken, beef, and sausage.
- Separate handling of raw and cooked meats.
- Sanitized cutting boards, knives, and preparation surfaces.
- Proper handwashing and glove practices.
- Accurate refrigeration and expiration records.
- Safe cooling of prepared foods.

Cooked foods being refrigerated for later use must be cooled from 135°F to 70°F within two hours and to 41°F or below within a total of six hours.

Mechanically tenderized beef cutlets require particular attention because tenderizing can transfer surface contamination into the interior of the meat.

The company should also maintain ingredient traceability so that each product can be associated with its supplier, manufacturing batch, and distribution destination.

The project should begin with products that require the fewest changes to existing restaurant operations.

| 5        | Sautéed onions                        | Low                    |

The first stage should involve testing the new menu products, measuring customer acceptance, and determining their actual preparation costs.

Only after the products have demonstrated demand should the company invest in more extensive manufacturing and distribution capabilities.

## **12. Conclusion: Expanding the existing menu without losing its character**

The strongest opportunity is to build upon the ingredients, flavors, and preparation methods that already make the restaurant successful.

The breaded, mechanically tenderized beef cutlet with gravy is a particularly appropriate addition because it represents a familiar Southern comfort-food product. It can be served with mashed potatoes, macaroni and cheese, broccoli, and cornbread.

The macaroni and cheese offers another major opportunity because its existing cheese sauce can support chopped steak, sausage, and chicken variations.

Sautéed onions, cheese mashed potatoes, broccoli, and six-piece cornbread muffin orders provide further menu enhancements without requiring an extensive new inventory.

The larger manufacturing proposal should follow the same philosophy: utilize common ingredients, standardize their preparation, automate repetitive activities, and distribute measured quantities to restaurants.

**The objective is to make more food choices possible from the ingredients already being purchased, while making the entire operation more efficient, consistent, and economical.**
