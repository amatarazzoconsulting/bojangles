# Global Fryer Manufacturers, Unit Costs, and Automation Payback Projections for Bojangles

## Executive Summary

This document provides a global manufacturer list for the integrated frying system specified in the Bojangles production plan, estimates per-unit costs for the fryer battery and the automation upgrades for each side of the service line, and presents financial projections for payback periods. The manufacturer landscape is dominated by three North American brands—**Henny Penny**, **Frymaster**, and **Pitco**—each with distinct strengths in chicken frying, oil management, and automation. Unit costs for a dual-vat fryer battery with the specified automation range from approximately **$29,000 to $55,000** depending on brand and configuration. The per-store automation upgrade for all sides (fryer, side preparation modules, service-line holding, data integration, and pickup infrastructure) is estimated at **$85,000 to $140,000**. Payback projections range from **2.5 to 4.5 years** depending on labor rates, oil savings, and throughput gains, with the most favorable economics in high-wage markets and high-volume stores.

## Part One: Global Manufacturers of Integrated Frying Systems

### 1.1 Primary North American Manufacturers

**Henny Penny** (Eaton, Ohio, USA) is the most directly relevant manufacturer for Bojangles because of its specialization in fried chicken equipment and its client list, which includes KFC, Wendy's, McDonald's, Chick-fil-A, and Chicken Licken . The company introduced reduced-oil-capacity technology with the Evolution Elite Open Fryer in 2009, using 30 pounds of oil per vat instead of the previous 50 pounds. The Evolution Elite series allows filtering of any vat at any time, a capability essential for Bojangles' filter-while-cooking requirement. The newer F5 Open Fryer (2023) features touchscreen controls and a three-minute filtering cycle, which is faster than most competitors . Henny Penny's pricing for a single-well Evolution Elite gas fryer with automatic oil top-off is approximately **$17,415** , while a double-tank gas unit (EEG-242) is approximately **$29,094** and a triple-tank gas unit (EEG-243) is approximately **$40,135** .

**Frymaster** (Shreveport, Louisiana, USA) is a brand of Welbilt and specializes in automated filtration and oil conservation. The FilterQuick series features automatic filtration, auto top-off (ATO), and optional oil quality sensors. Frymaster's Low-Oil-Volume (LOV) fryers cut oil usage by 40 percent and extend oil life by as much as 92 percent . Pricing for a two-vat FilterQuick gas fryer (2FQG30U, 30-pound vats) is approximately **$36,701** , while a three-vat unit (3FQG30U) is approximately **$48,735** . Frymaster's higher upfront cost reflects its more advanced automation components, but the oil savings and filtration consistency justify the investment in high-volume operations .

**Pitco** (Bow, New Hampshire, USA) is a brand of The Middleby Corporation with over 100 years of manufacturing history. Pitco's TorQ gas fryer combines forced convection heating with continuous in-line filtration, delivering up to a 10 percent reduction in cook times and 60 percent lower flue temperatures, with no filtration downtime compared to 5 to 7 minutes for a standard fryer . The ROV (Reduced Oil Volume) series uses 30 pounds of oil per vat instead of 50, with Auto Top Off and automatic filtration. The Solstice Supreme line reaches up to 70 percent thermal efficiency without a power blower . Pitco's SOS SmartOIL Sensor measures oil purity and remaining useful life, and combined with an ROV fryer can save up to 50 percent in oil costs . Pricing for a Solstice Supreme reduced-oil-volume two-fryer battery (SSHLV14TC-2/FD) is approximately **$54,683** , while a single system (SSHLV14C/184/FD) is approximately **$45,978** .

### 1.2 Secondary and Global Manufacturers

Beyond the three primary brands, several other manufacturers offer fryers with filtration and oil management capabilities relevant to the Bojangles specification. **Welbilt** (parent of Frymaster and other brands) and **The Middleby Corporation** (parent of Pitco) are the two dominant corporate entities in the commercial frying equipment market. **Electrolux Professional** and **Rational** offer European-engineered frying solutions, though their North American QSR penetration is lower than the three primary brands. **Ali Group** (parent of several foodservice equipment brands) has a presence in the fryer market through its subsidiary brands, but none with the chicken-specific specialization of Henny Penny.

For the waste oil recycling interface, **DAR PRO Solutions** and **Restaurant Technologies** are the two dominant North American service providers. DAR PRO connects directly to fryers for used cooking oil draining and provides real-time oil-level monitoring through its DarLinQ platform, with 90+ facilities and 2,100+ trucks covering over 90 percent of the U.S. population. Restaurant Technologies offers a fully managed Total Oil Management model with bulk fresh oil delivery, hands-free disposal, and used oil recycling, installing equipment at no upfront cost and servicing nationwide through 41 depots. For Bojangles, these are **service partners rather than equipment manufacturers**, but their interfaces must be specified in the fryer purchase to ensure compatibility.

### 1.3 Chinese Manufacturers for Cost-Sensitive Components

For the non-food-contact components of the automation system—the control board enclosure, the transfer cart frame, and the service-line holding units—Chinese manufacturers can provide cost savings of 30 to 50 percent compared to North American fabrication. However, the **frying host itself should not be sourced from a Chinese manufacturer** for the U.S. market unless the manufacturer holds NSF/ANSI 4 certification and UL/ETL listing, which is rare. The certification burden and the liability exposure of a non-certified fryer outweigh the cost savings. The Chinese manufacturers identified in earlier volumes (Dongguan Jiekexun, Shandong Kuaiba) are appropriate for the buffet holding equipment and the enclosure fabrication, but the fryer must come from a North American manufacturer with established certification and service networks.

## Part Two: Cost Estimates Per Unit and Per Store Group

### 2.1 Fryer Battery Cost Per Store

The **recommended configuration for Bojangles** is the dual-vat or triple-vat battery from Henny Penny or Frymaster, depending on the store's peak volume. The Henny Penny Evolution Elite series offers the most direct fit for chicken frying, with reduced oil capacity and filter-any-vat capability. The Frymaster FilterQuick series offers superior oil quality sensing and automatic top-off. The Pitco Solstice ROV offers the highest thermal efficiency and the strongest oil savings claim (up to 50 percent with the SmartOIL Sensor), but at a higher upfront cost.

For budgeting purposes, the **fryer battery cost per store** should be estimated at **$35,000 to $50,000** for a dual-vat or triple-vat configuration with the required automation features, including the waste oil discharge pump and connection point.

### 2.3 Cost Comparison: Full Automation vs. Fryer-Only Upgrade

For franchisees who want to phase the investment, a **fryer-only upgrade** (replacing the existing fryer with an automated filtration unit, without the side preparation modules or the full data platform) would cost approximately **$35,000 to $50,000 per store**. This captures the oil savings and the filtration consistency benefits while deferring the larger capital commitment. The payback on the fryer-only upgrade is faster (see Part Three), making it an attractive first step for cost-sensitive operators.

## Part Three: Financial Payback Projections

### 3.1 The Savings Drivers

The financial case for the automation upgrade rests on four savings drivers. **Oil savings** are the most significant and most immediate: Henny Penny's Evolution Elite uses 40 percent less oil than standard fryers, and Pitco's ROV with SmartOIL can save up to 50 percent in oil costs . For a store using 100 pounds of oil per week at $1.75 per pound, a 40 percent reduction saves approximately $3,640 per year. **Labor savings** from automated filtration and side preparation are estimated at 0.5 to 1.0 FTE per store, depending on volume. At a fully loaded labor cost of $18 to $22 per hour, the annual savings are $18,000 to $45,000, though the actual savings depend on whether the labor is redeployed to customer service rather than eliminated. **Waste reduction** from holding-time management and demand-based production is estimated at 2 to 5 percent of food cost, or $3,000 to $8,000 per year for a store with $500,000 in annual food purchases. **Energy savings** from induction heating and improved insulation in the side preparation and holding equipment are estimated at $1,500 to $3,000 per year.

The combined annual savings estimate for a fully automated store is **$26,000 to $60,000**, with a midpoint of approximately **$40,000**. The range reflects the variability in store volume, labor rates, and oil consumption.

### 3.2 Payback Period by Store Volume and Labor Market

The payback period for the $120,000 midpoint investment, at the midpoint annual savings of $40,000, is **3.0 years**. This aligns with the industry benchmark for restaurant kitchen automation systems, which typically achieve ROI within three to four years at current labor rates in major U.S. metros . The payback is faster in high-wage markets and higher-volume stores, and slower in low-wage markets and lower-volume stores.

| Scenario | Annual Savings | Payback Period (on $120,000) |
|----------|---------------|------------------------------|
| High-volume store, high-wage market ($22/hr labor) | $55,000–$60,000 | 2.0–2.2 years |
| Mid-volume store, mid-wage market ($18/hr labor) | $38,000–$42,000 | 2.9–3.2 years |
| Low-volume store, low-wage market ($15/hr labor) | $26,000–$30,000 | 4.0–4.6 years |
| Fryer-only upgrade (mid-volume, mid-wage) | $12,000–$15,000 | 2.5–3.5 years (on $40,000) |

The fryer-only upgrade has a **faster payback** because the capital cost is lower, even though the absolute savings are smaller. This makes it an attractive entry point for franchisees who are not ready to commit to the full automation package.

### 3.3 Sensitivity to Oil Prices and Labor Rates

The payback period is most sensitive to two variables: **oil prices** and **labor rates**. A 20 percent increase in oil prices would increase the oil savings by 20 percent, reducing the payback period by approximately 0.2 to 0.3 years for a mid-volume store. A $2 per hour increase in the fully loaded labor rate (from $18 to $20) would increase the labor savings by approximately $4,000 per year for a 1.0 FTE reduction, reducing the payback period by approximately 0.3 years. Conversely, a 20 percent decrease in oil prices or a $2 per hour decrease in labor rates would extend the payback by a similar amount.

The financial model should be updated quarterly with actual oil prices and labor rates from each market, and the payback projections should be recalculated for each store before the investment is approved. A store in a market where labor is scarce and wages are rising will have a more favorable payback than a store in a market where labor is abundant and wages are stagnant.

### 3.4 Group-Level Projections

For a group of 10 stores with a mix of high-, mid-, and low-volume locations, the blended annual savings would be approximately **$350,000 to $400,000**, and the blended payback period on a $1.2 million investment would be approximately **3.0 to 3.4 years**. For a group of 50 stores, the blended annual savings would be approximately **$1.75 million to $2.0 million**, and the payback period would be similar, assuming the same mix of store profiles. The group-level investment benefits from **volume purchasing discounts** on the fryer batteries and the side preparation modules, which could reduce the per-store cost by 5 to 10 percent, shortening the payback by 0.2 to 0.3 years.

The group-level projection should also account for the **learning curve** in installation and commissioning. The first few stores will require more engineering support and will have longer installation times. As the process matures and the installation crew gains experience, the per-store installation cost should decline by 10 to 15 percent, and the downtime should be reduced.

## Conclusion

The global manufacturer landscape for the Bojangles frying system is concentrated among three North American brands—Henny Penny, Frymaster, and Pitco—each with a viable product for the specification. Henny Penny offers the strongest fit for chicken frying; Frymaster offers the most advanced oil quality sensing; Pitco offers the highest thermal efficiency and the strongest oil savings claim. The per-store fryer cost ranges from $35,000 to $50,000, and the full automation upgrade for all sides ranges from $93,000 to $150,000. The payback period ranges from 2.0 to 4.6 years, depending on store volume and labor market, with a midpoint of approximately 3.0 years. The Main Ingredient Test applies to the financial case as it does to every other element of this plan: does the investment make the chicken more consistent, the oil last longer, or the operation more affordable? The oil savings and the filtration consistency do all three. The investment is justified not by the technology itself, but by the food that comes out of it and the cost that stays in the operator's pocket.
