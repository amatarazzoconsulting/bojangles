# Bojangles Front-Line Buffet Service Equipment: Engineering Department Manufacturing Specification

## Executive Summary

This document specifies the complete engineering requirements for a front-line buffet service device for Bojangles—a hot-holding and self-service station designed to present Bojangles sides (Dirty Rice, Macaroni & Cheese, Pinto Beans, Green Beans, Mashed Potatoes, Grits) alongside the Country Fried Steak Dinner components. The device is a **modular, NSF-compliant hot food holding and display unit** with integrated temperature control, sneeze guards, and a layout optimized for the dinner daypart. The document is written for an internal corporate engineering department tasked with either building this device in-house or contracting a Chinese manufacturer for production. The specifications cover every task, part, tool, material, and measurement required for manufacturing, and identify a specific Chinese company—**Dongguan Jiekexun Industrial Co., Ltd. (东莞市捷客迅实业有限公司)**—that has demonstrated capability in buffet line and heated holding equipment and is actively seeking North American market entry.

## Part One: The Front-Line Device—Design Requirements and Functional Specifications

### 1.1 Purpose and Operational Context

The front-line buffet device is designed to serve the Country Fried Steak Dinner and the existing Bojangles side menu in a self-service or staff-served format. It is not a replacement for the traditional counter service; it is an **adjacent station** positioned in the dining area or at the end of the counter, allowing customers to see the sides displayed in heated wells, select their portions, and complete their meal without returning to the main ordering counter. The device must maintain hot food at or above 135°F (57°C) as required by the FDA Food Code, with a thermometer accurate to ±3°F, and must protect food from contamination through sneeze guards or food shields . The device is not designed to cook or reheat food; it is a holding and display unit only, and this limitation must be clearly stated in the operator manual and on the equipment label .

The device must accommodate standard hotel pans (also called food pans or steam table pans) in full-size, half-size, and third-size configurations. The standard full-size pan is 20¾ inches by 12¾ inches, with depths of 2½, 4, or 6 inches. The device should have a minimum of **six full-size pan positions** to accommodate the side lineup: one for Dirty Rice, one for Macaroni & Cheese, one for Pinto Beans, one for Green Beans, one for Mashed Potatoes, and one for Grits. The Country Fried Steak itself should be held in a separate, smaller station or in a dedicated pan position, because the steak requires a different holding temperature and a different presentation than the sides. The device should include a **heated carving station or a dedicated steak holding pan** at one end, with a separate temperature control zone.

### 1.2 Temperature Zones and Control Architecture

The device should have **two independent temperature zones**. The primary zone holds the sides at 150°F to 165°F, which is the optimal range for maintaining both food safety and palatability. The secondary zone holds the Country Fried Steak at 145°F to 155°F, slightly lower to prevent the breading from drying out and the meat from becoming tough. Each zone should have its own thermostat, its own heating element, and its own temperature display. The controls should be **digital, with a visible set point and actual temperature**, and should include an audible alarm if the temperature drops below 135°F or rises above 180°F. The alarm should be loud enough to be heard over the ambient noise of a restaurant kitchen but not so loud that it disrupts the dining area. A mute button should be provided for temporary silencing, with automatic re-arming after five minutes.

The heating elements should be **electric, 208/240V single-phase**, with a total connected load of approximately 6,000 to 8,000 watts for a six-pan unit. The elements should be **tubular or calrod type**, mounted beneath the water pan in a wet-operation configuration. Wet operation is preferred over dry operation because water provides even heat distribution and prevents hot spots that can scorch the food. The water pan should be stainless steel, with a capacity of approximately 1.5 to 2 gallons, and should have a low-water cutoff sensor that shuts off the heating element if the water level drops below a safe threshold. The device should include a **drain valve** at the lowest point of the water pan for easy emptying and cleaning.

### 1.3 Construction Materials and Sanitation Requirements

All food-contact surfaces must be **304 stainless steel**, which is the industry standard for commercial food equipment and is required for NSF/ANSI 2 certification . The exterior surfaces may be 304 or 430 stainless steel, depending on cost considerations, but the food zone—the pan rails, the water pan, the sneeze guard supports, and any surface that may contact food—must be 304. The device must be **easily cleanable**, with smooth welds, rounded corners, and no crevices where food particles or moisture can accumulate . The legs must be adjustable, with a minimum height of 6 inches from the floor to allow cleaning beneath the unit, and must be fitted with sanitary footings that are resistant to corrosion and cleaning chemicals.

The sneeze guard must be **glass or polycarbonate**, at least ¼ inch thick, and must be positioned to intercept the direct line between the customer's mouth and the food on display . The guard should extend across the full length of the food display and should be angled or curved to provide protection without obstructing access. The guard must be removable for cleaning, with stainless steel brackets and thumb screws rather than tools. The guard should meet the sightline requirements of local health codes, which typically require the guard to extend from a point at least 6 inches above the top of the food to a point at least 12 inches beyond the front edge of the food display.

### 1.4 Dimensional Specifications

The overall dimensions of the device should be designed to fit within a standard Bojangles dining area or counter footprint. The recommended dimensions are:

| Dimension | Specification | Rationale |

| Overall length | 96 inches (8 feet) | Accommodates six full-size pans plus a steak station with spacing |

The joints between sections should be sealed with a food-grade silicone gasket to prevent water and food debris from entering.

### 2.2 The Manufacturing Process—Step by Step

The manufacturing process for the front-line buffet device follows a sequence of fabrication, assembly, wiring, and testing operations. Each step requires specific tools and skills, and the quality of the final product depends on the precision of each step.

**Step 1: Metal Fabrication.** The stainless steel sheets are cut to size using a **CNC plasma cutter or a laser cutter**. The cut pieces are then formed using a **press brake** with the appropriate dies to create the pan rails, the water pan, and the body panels. The tolerance for the formed parts is ±0.5 mm. The formed parts are deburred using a **deburring tool or a grinding wheel** to remove sharp edges and prepare the surfaces for welding. All food-zone surfaces must be smooth and free of pits, cracks, or crevices.

**Step 2: Welding.** The body frame, the water pan, and the pan rails are welded using **TIG (tungsten inert gas) welding** with 308L stainless steel filler rod. TIG welding is preferred over MIG welding because it produces cleaner welds with less spatter and better penetration control, which is essential for food equipment. The welds must be **continuous and leak-tight**, particularly on the water pan, which must hold water without leaking. After welding, the welds are **ground flush and polished** to a smooth finish that is easy to clean. Any weld that cannot be ground flush (such as interior corners) must be smooth and continuous, with no undercuts or crevices.

**Step 3: Surface Finishing.** The welded assembly is **passivated** to restore the corrosion resistance of the stainless steel that is depleted by welding and grinding. Passivation involves dipping the assembly in a nitric or citric acid bath, rinsing with deionized water, and drying. The surface finish should be **#4 brushed finish** on exterior surfaces and **2B mill finish** on interior surfaces that are not visible, as long as those surfaces are smooth and cleanable.

**Step 4: Electrical Assembly.** The electrical enclosure is mounted to the frame, and the heating elements, thermostats, sensors, and power cords are installed. The wiring must follow **NFPA 70 (National Electrical Code)** and must be rated for the temperature and moisture environment of the device. All connections must be made with **crimp terminals or terminal blocks**, not wire nuts, which can loosen under vibration. The wiring must be **color-coded** (black for hot, white for neutral, green for ground) and must be secured with **cable ties or conduit** to prevent movement and abrasion. The electrical enclosure must be **grounded** to the frame with a dedicated grounding conductor.

**Step 5: Control Panel Assembly.** The digital thermostats and the temperature displays are mounted in the control panel face, and the wiring is connected to the sensors and the heating elements. The controls must be **labeled clearly** with the zone designation (Sides or Steak), the set point, and the alarm limits. The control panel must be **accessible for service** without removing the entire device from its location, and the components must be **replaceable** with standard tools.

**Step 6: Sneeze Guard Installation.** The sneeze guard brackets are mounted to the frame, and the glass or polycarbonate panels are installed. The panels must be **level and square**, with no gaps between the panels and the brackets that could trap food or moisture. The panels must be **removable** for cleaning, and the brackets must be designed so that the panels can be lifted out without tools. The guard must be positioned so that it does not interfere with the customer's access to the food but does intercept the direct line from the customer's mouth to the food.

**Step 7: Leak Testing.** The water pan is filled with water and allowed to sit for **at least one hour** to check for leaks. Any leak must be repaired by re-welding and re-testing. The drain valve must be operated to confirm that it opens and closes smoothly and does not leak when closed.

**Step 8: Electrical Safety Testing.** The completed device is tested for **ground continuity, insulation resistance, and dielectric withstand** (hipot) in accordance with UL 197 or the applicable standard for commercial electric cooking appliances. The heating elements are energized and the thermostats are calibrated to confirm that they maintain the set point within ±5°F. The low-water cutoff is tested by draining the water pan and confirming that the heating element shuts off. The alarm is tested by simulating an over-temperature condition.

**Step 9: Final Inspection and Packaging.** The device is inspected for **cosmetic defects** (scratches, dents, weld discoloration), **functional defects** (loose connections, misaligned panels, sticking valves), and **labeling compliance** (NSF mark, electrical rating, warning labels). The device is then wrapped in protective film, placed on a shipping pallet, and secured with straps. The packaging must be designed to protect the device during ocean freight and inland trucking, with **foam corner protectors, stretch wrap, and a wooden crate** for export shipments.

## Part Three: Chinese Manufacturing Partner—Dongguan Jiekexun Industrial Co., Ltd.

### 3.1 Company Profile and Capabilities

**Dongguan Jiekexun Industrial Co., Ltd. (东莞市捷客迅实业有限公司)**, operating under the brand **Jikeson / 捷客迅**, is a commercial kitchen equipment manufacturer based in Dongguan, Guangdong Province, China. The company was founded in 2008 (some sources list 2016 for a related entity) and has approximately 50–100 employees . Its core competency is **commercial induction heating equipment and buffet service lines**, including heated holding tables, self-service weighing buffet lines, and modular serving stations. The company explicitly markets **self-service weighing heated buffet lines (自助称重保温餐线)** for community canteens, schools, factories, and restaurants, and it offers **custom, non-standard dimensions** and **modular拼接 (splicing)** to fit different site layouts .

Jiekexun's buffet line products are directly relevant to the Bojangles front-line device. The company's standard buffet line includes **304 stainless steel food pans, dust-proof insulated covers, zone temperature controllers, electric heating plates, high-precision weighing sensors, price display screens, and drainage trays** . The heating system maintains food at **60–70°C (140–158°F)**, which is within the FDA Food Code hot-holding range, and the company claims the system prevents food from drying out or cooling throughout the day . The company also offers **landing-style and embedded-style** configurations, which means the device can be freestanding or built into a counter .

### 3.2 Certification Status and the North American Market Gap

This is the critical issue for Bojangles. Jiekexun's products carry **Chinese domestic certifications (3C, ISO 9001)** and some **international certifications (CE, UL for certain product lines)**, but the company's buffet line products are **not currently listed in the NSF certification database** for NSF/ANSI 2 (Food Equipment) or NSF/ANSI 4 (Commercial Cooking, Rethermalization, and Powered Hot Food Holding Equipment) . For a device to be legally installed in a Bojangles restaurant in the United States, it must have **NSF/ANSI 4 certification** for hot food holding equipment, and it must have **NRTL listing (UL, ETL, or CSA)** for electrical safety . Without these certifications, the device cannot pass a local health department inspection, regardless of how well it is built.

The good news is that **NSF International has testing capabilities in China** and can manage the certification process for Chinese manufacturers seeking North American market access . Jiekexun, or any Chinese manufacturer Bojangles selects, would need to submit the device for **NSF/ANSI 4 testing** (hot holding performance, temperature accuracy, sanitation design) and **UL 197 or UL 60335 testing** (electrical safety). The certification process typically takes **18 to 26 weeks** if electrical and sanitation testing are done sequentially, or shorter if an integrated certification provider is used . The cost of certification is borne by the manufacturer or the importer, and it is a prerequisite for the device to be sold in the North American market.

### 3.3 Why Jiekexun Is a Candidate—and What Must Be Verified

Jiekexun is a **candidate for further due diligence**, not a confirmed supplier. The company has demonstrated the following capabilities that align with the Bojangles requirement: (1) it manufactures heated buffet lines with zone temperature control, which is the core function of the front-line device; (2) it offers **custom dimensions and modular configurations**, which means it can adapt a standard design to Bojangles' 96-inch specification; (3) it uses **304 stainless steel** in its food-contact surfaces, which is the required material for NSF certification; and (4) it has experience serving **chain restaurants and institutional canteens**, which suggests familiarity with volume production and commercial durability requirements .

However, several factors must be verified before a manufacturing agreement is signed. First, **certification**: Jiekexun must either already hold NSF/ANSI 4 certification for its buffet line products or be willing to obtain it before the first production run. Bojangles should not accept a "certification pending" promise without a contractual commitment and a timeline. Second, **factory audit**: Bojangles engineering should conduct an on-site audit of Jiekexun's Dongguan facility to verify welding quality, material traceability (confirming that 304 stainless steel is used, not 201 or 430 in food zones), electrical assembly practices, and quality control procedures. Third, **prototype testing**: a single prototype unit should be built and shipped to the United States for independent testing by a qualified laboratory, including temperature performance, electrical safety, and sanitation design review. Only after these three steps should a production order be placed.

An alternative Chinese manufacturer with **existing NSF/ANSI 2 certification** is **Shandong Kuaiba Kitchen Equipment Co., Ltd. (KUARBARR)**, which is listed in the NSF database for worktables (NSF/ANSI 2) . While Kuaiba's listed products are worktables rather than heated buffet lines, the company has demonstrated the ability to pass NSF certification, which is a significant advantage. Bojangles engineering should contact both companies and compare their willingness and capability to meet the NSF/ANSI 4 requirement.

## Part Four: Integration with the Bojangles Service Line and Operational Workflow

### 4.1 Physical Placement and Utility Connections

The front-line device should be positioned in the dining area, at the end of the main counter, or in a dedicated buffet zone. The location must have access to **208/240V single-phase power** (two dedicated circuits, one per temperature zone, each rated at 30 amps), a **water supply** for filling the water pan (a simple pot-filler faucet or a hose connection is sufficient), and a **floor drain** for emptying the water pan during cleaning. The device should be positioned so that the customer approach is from one side and the staff replenishment access is from the other side, creating a **dual-sided workflow** that keeps customers and staff separated during service. The aisle in front of the device must be at least **36 inches wide** to meet accessibility requirements .

### 4.2 Staffing and Replenishment Workflow

The device is designed for **staff-assisted self-service**: the customer selects their own sides, but a staff member is stationed behind the device to replenish pans, monitor temperatures, and assist customers who need help. The replenishment workflow uses **back-up pans** that are filled in the kitchen and swapped into the device when the front pan reaches approximately one-third full. The empty pan is returned to the kitchen for washing and refilling. This workflow requires the device to have **pan rails that allow pans to be slid in and out easily**, with enough clearance so that a full pan can be lifted without scraping the adjacent pan. The pan rails should be **adjustable** to accommodate different pan depths (2½, 4, or 6 inches), and the device should include **pan dividers** that can be removed to create larger or smaller compartments as the menu changes.

### 4.3 Cleaning and Maintenance

The device must be cleaned daily. The cleaning procedure includes: (1) turning off the power and allowing the device to cool; (2) draining the water pan through the drain valve; (3) removing the pans, pan rails, and sneeze guard panels; (4) washing all food-contact surfaces with a food-safe detergent and a soft brush or cloth; (5) rinsing with clean water; (6) sanitizing with a food-safe sanitizer (quaternary ammonium or chlorine-based); (7) allowing all surfaces to air-dry before reassembly. The heating elements should be inspected weekly for scale buildup, and the water pan should be delimed monthly with a food-safe descaler. The thermostats should be calibrated quarterly against a calibrated reference thermometer. These maintenance tasks should be documented in a log that is reviewed by the store manager.

## Conclusion

The front-line buffet device is a straightforward piece of commercial food equipment—a heated holding and display unit with two temperature zones, NSF-compliant construction, and a modular design that fits the Bojangles dinner service model. The engineering requirements are not exotic: 304 stainless steel, electric tubular heating elements, digital thermostats, and a glass sneeze guard. The manufacturing process is well within the capabilities of a Chinese contract manufacturer, and **Dongguan Jiekexun Industrial Co., Ltd.** has the relevant product experience and custom fabrication capability to be a candidate. The critical path is **certification**: the device must obtain NSF/ANSI 4 and NRTL listing before it can be installed in a U.S. Bojangles, and this requirement must be written into the manufacturing contract with milestones and penalties for non-compliance. The device does not need to be invented from scratch; it needs to be specified precisely, certified rigorously, and integrated into the service line with a clear staffing and replenishment workflow. The Main Ingredient Test applies: does this device make the sides hotter, the presentation more appetizing, or the customer's experience more satisfying? A properly designed and certified holding unit does all three. A unit that fails certification or holds food at the wrong temperature does none.
